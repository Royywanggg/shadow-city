# Opportunity and cumulative hazard

The `typed-crime-v1` model distinguishes legal spatial co-presence, a social encounter, an offence-specific opportunity, an attempt and a completed offence. A social conversation is not required for an opportunity. The `querySpatialCandidates` index supplies one-second samples; typed person-targeted actions require the **same surface**, three-dimensional distance ≤ 1.8 m, and an available actor and victim. Open-portal proximity alone is insufficient. Theft additionally requires a pre-existing accessible item currently possessed by that victim. A place resource is not silently treated as an item.

Each directed actor–victim–type–item–surface tuple creates a stable episode. The episode records start/end Tick, eligible seconds, surface, level, place, last position, risk, guardianship, hazard, threshold and end reason. Two Ticks without contact end it. Transferring the item invalidates other theft episodes. The bounded recent summary retains the latest configured count; replay from the same seed/config/commands reconstructs earlier summaries at a historical Tick. The Event Explorer displays only summaries actually available in the inspected state.

For a type-specific rate `r` in attempts per eligible exposure hour, each legal second adds `r / 3600` to cumulative hazard `H`. A seed-keyed `U ∈ (0,1)` fixes the episode threshold `T = -ln(U)`. An attempt occurs on first crossing `H ≥ T`. Under constant `λ = r / 3600`, the mathematically implied attempt probability after `t` eligible seconds is `1 − exp(−λt)`. Zero rate or zero exposure yields zero probability. Theft and assault have separate cause-specific thresholds; if both cross within the same sampled second, the earlier fractional crossing wins. Other simultaneous actions resolve by stable episode ID, then revalidate current world state.

The synthetic rate multiplier uses the actor's seeded propensity for the relevant base category, the existing district/time exposure, perceived risk, reward and the observed nearby guardian count. It uses only fields already present in the model. The formula is in `src/simulation/crime/engine.ts`; the cap of 100 attempts per eligible hour prevents numerical explosion. Base rates are **synthetic assumptions**, not empirical estimates. A fixed-contact experiment tests the cumulative-hazard equation independently of changing city trajectories. City experiments report eligible episode seconds, attempts and completed offences separately; denominators must not be confused with resident-hours.

The one-second sampling grid is an approximation: a contact shorter than one second may be missed. This limitation is distinct from the exact constant-rate hazard formula. Duplicate residents on different floors cannot create an opportunity from matching X/Z coordinates.
