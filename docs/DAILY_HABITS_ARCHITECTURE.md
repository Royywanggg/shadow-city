# Daily habits and adaptive routine architecture

This is an incremental, synthetic behaviour extension of the existing integrated `WorldModelV2` and `SimulationStateV5`. It does not replace navigation, encounter, offending, the character assets, or the old replay path. `SimulationConfigV5.habit` is optional and fingerprinted; an omitted habit config runs the prior v5 behaviour. The current integrated app enables the extension by default and `?habits=off` opens the old behaviour for comparison.

## Data ownership and Tick order

`WorldModelV2` remains the static authority for semantic places, opening hours, slots, surfaces and navigation edges. `HabitProfileV1` is immutable per citizen and comes from a separate seed and agent ID. `CitizenHabitStateV1` stores current needs, one intention, provisional daily goals, learned strengths/valence and immutable chunked records of decisions, activity outcomes and updates. `SimulationStateV5` owns that state, actual citizen positions, reservations, interaction state, events and commands. React reads state; it does not decide activities.

For a habit-enabled Tick, the engine applies access commands, derives calendar opening restrictions, reads **previous-Tick** memories/relationships/crowding, updates needs and day transitions, then performs one resident decision when their current commitment ends or a place must evacuate. It reserves a legal slot, follows the existing 3D graph, records arrival/dwell, and only then runs opportunity/encounter/offender processing. New encounter feedback can affect a later Tick, never recursively trigger a second decision in the same Tick. The selected route and `CitizenStateV5` position remain authoritative; animation is a read-only representation of activity, heading and movement mode.

The synthetic calendar is `startHour × 3600 + Tick × calendarSecondsPerTick`, modulo 86,400 seconds per day. The default is **30 calendar seconds per Tick**, so there are 2,880 Ticks per day and, from the default 08:00 start, day 1 begins at Tick 1,920. Physical movement, second-resolution contact and exposure remain **6 simulated seconds per Tick**. The 5:1 calendar scale lets a resident cross the 300 m city within a plausible share of a planned day without changing the established movement or encounter model. It is an explicit modelling approximation, not a single physically consistent clock. `Date.now()` and rendering FPS do not enter decisions. Speed controls change how often Ticks advance, not a given Tick result.

## Profiles, needs and decisions

`createHabitProfilesV1` derives wake/rest preference, work windows only for residents with an existing workplace, preferred places, activity preferences, sociability, regularity, willingness to change, shopping cadence and typical dwell from `habit.seed + agentId + purpose`. It does not read appearance, clothing or offending propensity. Home and workplace IDs come from the existing profile. The current world has no school category, so a fictitious student occupation is not created.

The decision function enumerates compatible semantic places and activity kinds. A candidate must be open, reachable on the existing navigation graph and have a free activity slot. Work has a time-window obligation term. The utility is the integer sum of `needs + obligation + learned habit + stable preference + relevant social familiarity + personal place valence + time fit + keyed variation − travel cost − prior crowd − personally perceived risk − interruption cost`. The individual terms and infeasible reasons are retained in the actual `ActivityDecisionTraceV1`; the highest feasible score wins, with a stable ID tie break. Keyed variation is derived from habit seed, citizen, day, decision ordinal, activity and place, so adding or excluding an unrelated candidate does not consume a shared random stream. There is no rule that forces a specific trip, bridge, interaction or crime at a fixed Tick.

The default utility coefficients are in `src/simulation/habits/decision.ts`: maximum needs 300, work obligation 260, learned term 160 × `habitWeight` × regularity, personal preference 120 plus preferred-place bonus 30, social 45 plus up to 45 from a **completed personal interaction at that place**, valence ±80 × `feedbackWeight`, time fit 100, risk up to −100 × `feedbackWeight`, travel up to −170, crowd up to −90, interruption up to −120 and keyed variation −30…30. Travel uses the existing millisecond route cost and personal traveller costs. These are synthetic and uncalibrated weights. A decision is taken when the previous activity ends, a closed place requires evacuation, or a short wait expires; ongoing activity is not silently overwritten.

The current daily goals are provisional rest, three eating windows, and a work window for employed residents on their generated workdays. They carry a priority and a state. They are refreshed at the calendar day boundary, which is distinct from session Reset to Tick 0. The engine retains only one active intention per resident. An intention may be travelling, active, delayed, interrupted, cancelled or completed; actual activity history is recorded only after a state transition. A planned social outing is a public-place trip; it becomes a social success only if the existing Encounter system records a completed interaction. Otherwise it is logged as leisure, without social-habit reinforcement.

## Adaptation and space

Completing an activity moves its learned activity strength 4.5% toward 1,000 and its destination strength 4% toward 1,000. A failed destination reduces that place strength to 92% of its prior value. At a day boundary, activity and place strengths decay 2%/1.5% toward baseline 200; place valence decays 2% toward neutral 500. A new previous-Tick **personal** memory changes valence by up to ±80, weighted by confidence and salience. The exact before/after/source/rule is stored in `HabitChangeV1`, and the next decision consumes the changed values. Existing directed familiarity and personally perceived place risk enter only relevant candidate terms. No global ground truth is injected into a resident's subjective preference.

Capacity competition uses the existing deterministic per-Tick priority, reserved slots and road/portal graph. Public places can fill; a resident then picks another feasible place or waits briefly. Closure commands and automatic hours prevent new entry. Occupants can leave through legal exit portals. In particular, a closed Galleria shop may cross the closed central hall on its way out; that is an egress exception, not permission to enter the shut shop. A resident on a blocked route finishes a safe segment, replans from its node or records a failed trip. No activity teleports a resident or creates a renderer-only position.

## God View, replay and metrics

The researcher sidebar adds Routine, Today, Decisions and Habits tabs. They read `HabitProfileV1`, `SimulationStateV5.habits`, real citizen and encounter events, and actual decision traces. Today labels the goals provisional and separates them from recorded activity and interaction history. Decision candidates and scores are never reconstructed by UI copy. The inspector remains independent of Truth/Observed/Compare and the restricted `ObserverPort`; habit internals are not added to report payloads. Seek/replay recomputes from the fingerprinted config and ordered commands; Reset returns to the exact Tick 0 state with the session's commands preserved.

`summarizeDailyHabitsV1` reports complete-day activity counts/minutes, trips in motion and calendar units, destination diversity/repeat visits, cancellation rate, work-start deviation from preferred start, strength changes and **actual** social interactions. `summarizeRoutineRegularityV1` compares observed activity at each hour midpoint of two complete calendar days; travel and unclassified hours are excluded from its denominator. Metrics never assume a planned event happened. The experiment script runs three paired seeds for habit-weight and place-closure interventions and writes the raw outputs under `docs/screenshots/`.

## Model boundaries

The activity calendar and physical contact clock intentionally use different scales. The synthetic weights, schedule preferences, need rates, memory effects and adaptation rates have not been calibrated to a real city. Decision traces and event arrays grow with experiment length; immutable chunks reduce replay-copy cost but do not bound state size. The app has no school place, seat/gesture state or authenticated police observer. Full sitting and conversation gestures therefore remain visual work for a future animation-specific task. No claim about real-world causality follows from these counterfactual runs.
